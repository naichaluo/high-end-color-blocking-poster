# Torn Paper Relief Poster

## Purpose

Create a polished commercial key visual inspired by premium paper-set photography: pale paper fields, irregular torn edges, layered relief cards, soft cast shadows, and a carefully staged hero product. It should feel like a designed advertisement, not a scrapbook or children's craft project.

This skill is independent from `high-end-color-blocking-poster` and `paper-collage-poster`. Do not combine their rules unless explicitly requested.

## Trigger conditions

Use for torn-paper, paper-relief, paper-set, tactile layered commercial posters, or soft natural product advertisements with raised paper panels and dimensional staging. Do not use for flat vector art, generic collage, normal poster requests, or palette-only work.

## Non-negotiable decisions

1. Lock product, brand, offer, price, dates, URL, QR code, legal copy, approved wording, and supplied logos. Never invent or stylize these as model-generated text.
2. Use a restrained palette: warm paper white or pale sage as field, one product accent, and one dark text accent. Avoid loud multi-color blocking.
3. Make torn paper structural: use 2–4 deliberate ripped bands or windows with visible fibers and slight curl; do not scatter random scraps.
4. Build physical depth: background sheet → torn opening/window → rear scenery → hero product → foreground props → offer card and typography overlays. Keep one soft shadow direction.
5. Use one hero product cluster. Secondary objects should explain flavor, use, or benefit.
6. Reserve clean zones for exact Chinese copy, logos, numbers, QR codes, and disclaimers. Typeset locked information after image generation.

## Workflow

1. Extract audience, product, promise, mandatory copy, CTA, output ratio, brand assets, and requested deliverable. Inspect the source poster's hierarchy first.
2. State: “The poster turns **[core message]** into **[tactile paper scene]**, so the audience understands **[benefit/action]**.”
3. Define paper weight/surface, torn-edge character, relief depth, shadow direction, palette, and lighting. Prefer matte stock, deckled fibers, subtle embossing, paper grain, and soft studio light; avoid glossy plastic, chrome, glassmorphism, and hard CGI bevels.
4. Return: creative angle; locked versus reinterpreted elements; a 5–7 layer stack; composition and text-safe zones; visual-only prompt; post-production overlay list; negative prompt.
5. When generating, use the supplied poster as reference. Preserve product silhouette, packaging geometry, mascot presence, offer-card location, and hierarchy while changing material treatment. Inspect edges, shadows, product count, logos, typography artifacts, and overlay zones.

Read `references/torn-paper-relief-method.md` for detailed composition and prompt guidance.

## Quality gate

- Product, headline zone, and offer/CTA remain obvious at thumbnail size.
- Torn paper reads as designed structure, not damage or random scrapbook texture.
- Layers have coherent order, contact shadows, and believable thickness.
- Palette is quiet, warm, and commercial; accents support the product.
- No invented facts, text, logos, prices, URLs, dates, or QR codes appear.

## Scope boundary

Do not silently merge this skill with `high-end-color-blocking-poster`, `paper-collage-poster`, or another style skill. If a hybrid is requested, name the combination and identify each skill's decisions.
